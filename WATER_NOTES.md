# Water rendering

The lagoon combines its earlier continuous wave behavior with the newer water renderer inspired by Evan Wallace's [WebGL Water](https://github.com/evanw/webgl-water). The original sources and previous numerical solver port remain in `references/webgl-water/`; that pool-style solver is no longer active. See [THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md](THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md).

`src/waves.js` restores the four directional, continuously flowing wave layers and expanding click, swimming and rain rings. Rings spread at 1.7 world units per simulation second, fade, and disappear by nine seconds. Their frequency is limited for the texture resolution, and the final two seconds taper smoothly. Wave height and surface detail affect the motion field directly. Flow speed controls wave time, rain and wakes together; pausing or setting flow to zero freezes automatic water behavior. Reset clears the rings and restarts the wave phase.

A single 256 × 256 half-float texture stores height and normals using the newer renderer's layout. It renders once when time, settings, camera coverage or ripple inputs change; frozen still-frame exports reuse it. World-space evaluation and integer-texel shifts keep waves anchored while the camera travels. Continuous motion has no accumulated simulation history, random camera-relative wind drops, or startup ripple burst. This uses about 0.5 MiB for the wave texture, with no catch-up simulation passes.

The newer iterative normal lookup, transparent water, Fresnel mixing, underwater refraction and reflected-depth sampling remain. Water lighting remains disabled: no caustic render target, caustic material samples, or surface sun-glare term. Ordinary scene lighting still makes the island and creatures visible. This is a visual wave model with approximate scene optics, not full fluid dynamics.

The demo's complete renderer cannot be dropped into a lagoon unchanged: it analytically intersects a box pool and a sphere. Here, reflected/refracted rays instead sample the island's scene color and depth buffers. Above-water refraction projects the difference between a flat-water and wave-normal Snell ray, scaled by scene depth. The screen-space approximation fades at viewport edges and rejects dry foreground objects; it does not attempt full offscreen parallax. Both sides use an air/water index ratio of 1 / 1.333 (inverse underwater), normals facing the incident medium, and total internal reflection. Fresnel reflectance uses the dielectric equations rather than the demo's deliberately stronger artistic mix.

The underwater reflection follows the reflected direction through a mirrored depth buffer and applies absorption to both underwater ray segments. Refraction samples the sky and above-water scene depth. Offscreen objects fall back to water/sky colors; these remain screen-space approximations, not unrestricted ray tracing. Camera crossings immediately invalidate the reflection cache, including when animation is paused.

Validation: `node wave-check.mjs` checks continuous motion without input, wave controls, pause, world anchoring, ripple speed and decay, reset, finite output and consistent time sampling. `node detail-check.mjs` captures the island, underwater reef and moving underside. `node smoke.mjs` checks controls, diving/resurfacing, lighting presets, export and mobile layout. `node habitat-check.mjs` exercises collision-aware animal motion.
